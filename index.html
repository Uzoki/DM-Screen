    Carrying & Encumbrance

    A Small or Medium creature can carry 15 × its Strength score in pounds, and can drag, push, or lift twice that amount. Each size category above Medium doubles both totals; a Tiny creature halves them.

    Carrying Capacity

    Situation/SizeCalculationStr = 10
    Carry Capacity15 x Str [10]150 lbs.
    Drag, Push, Lift2x or 30 x Str [10]300 lbs.

    Encumbrance (Optional Rule)

    Carrying more than 5 × Strength imposes the Encumbered condition (Speed −10 feet). Carrying more than 10 × Strength, up to the maximum carrying capacity, imposes Heavily Encumbered instead (Speed −20 feet, plus Disadvantage on Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution ability checks, attack rolls, and saving throws).

    Situation/SizeCalculationStr = 10Penalty

    Downtime Activities
    Buy a Magic Item

    A character can spend downtime searching for a magic item to buy. This requires at least one workweek and 100 gp in expenses, covering the cost of maintaining a wealthier lifestyle needed to make contact with sellers. Spending extra money or time improves the search: for every additional 100 gp spent beyond the initial 100, or every additional workweek spent beyond the first, the character gains a cumulative +1 bonus on their Charisma (Persuasion) check — up to a maximum combined bonus of +10.

    Search bonus: +0 (from money/time spent, capped at +10 combined)

    Buying a Magic Item Check

    The Persuasion check total determines how many magic items are available, and which of the nine Magic Item Tables (A through I) the DM rolls on for each — higher totals give access to more valuable and more numerous items.

    Check TotalItems Available
    1-5Roll 1d6 times on Magic Item Table A
    6-10Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table B
    11-15Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table C
    16-20Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table D
    21-25Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table E
    26-30Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table F
    31-35Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table G
    36-40Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table H
    41+Roll 1d4 times on Magic Item Table I

    Magic Item Price

    Pricing magic items is a matter of debate among D&D players. The DMG bases price on an item's rarity, though rarity isn't always a reliable measure of an item's true power. The table below gives the asking price by rarity as described in Xanathar's Guide to Everything.

    RarityAsking Price*
    Common(1d6 + 1) x 10 gp
    Uncommon1d6 x 100 gp
    Rare2d10 x 1,000 gp
    Very Rare(1d4 + 1) x 10,000 gp
    Legendary2d6 x 25,000 gp

    * Halved for a consumable item like a potion or scroll.

    Magic Item Purchase Complication (Optional)

    The magic item trade is fraught with peril — the large sums of money involved, and the power offered by magic items, attract thieves, con artists, and other villains. Unlike most downtime activities, Xanathar's Guide to Everything makes complications for buying a magic item entirely optional; roll on the table below (or invent your own) only if you want to make things more interesting.

    d12Complication
    1The item is a fake, planted by an enemy.†
    2The item is stolen by the party's enemies.†
    3The item is cursed by a god.
    4The item's original owner will kill to reclaim it; the party's enemies spread news of its sale.†
    5The item is at the center of a dark prophecy.
    6The seller is murdered before the sale.
    7The seller is a devil looking to make a bargain.
    8The item is the key to freeing an evil entity.
    9A third party bids on the item, doubling its price.†
    10The item is an enslaved, intelligent entity.
    11The item is tied to a cult.
    12The party's enemies spread rumors that the item is an artifact of evil.†

    † Might involve a rival.

    Carousing

    A character can spend downtime carousing — throwing parties, drinking, gossiping, and making contacts among a chosen social class. Carousing requires 1 workweek and an amount of gold based on the social class the character carouses among.

    Carousing Results

    Check TotalResult
    1-5The character has made a hostile contact.
    6-10The character makes no new contacts.
    11-15The character makes one allied contact.
    16-20The character makes two allied contacts.
    21+The character makes three allied contacts.

    Carousing Complications

    Each workweek spent carousing brings a 10% chance of a complication, drawn from a table specific to the social class carousing was done among.

    Lower Class

    d8Complication
    1You awaken in an unfamiliar place with no memory of the previous night, and 2d6 x 5 gp lighter.
    2A vagrant recognizes you and begins to follow you around, asking for money and trying to protect you.
    3You wake up in the wrong bed, with an angry spouse standing over you.
    4You gain a reputation as a rabble-rouser. Guards keep a close eye on you for the next 1d4 downtime periods.
    5You are enlisted, willingly or not, to help with a criminal task (moving contraband, acting as a lookout, or similar).†
    6A brawl breaks out. Succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity or Strength check (your choice) or take 1d6 damage and spend one additional workweek recovering.
    7You are robbed, and lose 1d6 x 5 gp. Succeed on a DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check to notice and prevent it.
    8You make a lifelong friend (or enemy) of someone important to the local community.†

    Middle Class

    d8Complication
    1You awaken in an unfamiliar place with no memory of the previous night, and 2d6 x 10 gp lighter.
    2You gain a reputation as an eccentric. Merchants overcharge you by 10% for the next 1d4 downtime periods.
    3You wake up in the wrong bed, with an angry spouse standing over you.
    4A merchant tries to rope you into a risky business venture.†
    5You're challenged to a duel of wit, cards, or dice by a local notable. Succeed on a DC 15 check (your choice of relevant skill) or lose 2d6 x 10 gp.
    6A brawl breaks out at a tavern or gambling hall you're in. Succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity or Strength check (your choice) or take 1d6 damage.
    7You're pickpocketed for 1d6 x 10 gp. Succeed on a DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check to notice and prevent it.
    8You make a lifelong friend (or enemy) of a prominent local merchant or official.†

    Upper Class

    d8Complication
    1You awaken in an unfamiliar place with no memory of the previous night, and 2d6 x 20 gp lighter.
    2You become entangled in a noble's scheme or scandal, whether you want to be or not.†
    3You wake up in the wrong bed, with an angry (and powerful) spouse standing over you.
    4A noble tries to recruit you for a dangerous, clandestine task.†
    5You're challenged to a formal duel over a perceived slight. Succeed on a DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion) check to talk your way out of it.
    6You're accused of a crime you didn't commit (theft, cheating, insulting a noble house).†
    7Your extravagant spending draws the attention of thieves. Succeed on a DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check or lose 2d6 x 20 gp to a pickpocket.
    8You make a lifelong friend (or enemy) of a powerful noble.†

    † Might involve a rival.

    Crime

    A character can spend downtime committing a crime — robbing a mark chosen ahead of time. This requires 1 workweek of preparation and surveillance, and no gold up front. The character picks a target, then makes three checks: a Dexterity (Stealth) check, a Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) check, and a third check of the player's choice (Intelligence (Investigation), Wisdom (Perception), or Charisma (Deception), depending on how the crime is being pulled off) — all three against the same DC, set by the target chosen.

    Crime Results

    SuccessesResult
    3The character earns the target's full loot value.
    2The character earns half the target's loot value.
    1The heist fails, but the character escapes unnoticed.
    0The character is caught. They must pay a fine equal to the target's full loot value, and spend one workweek in jail for every 25 gp owed (unless the fine is paid).

    Crime Complications

    Even a well-planned crime can go wrong. There's a 10% chance of a complication for the workweek spent planning and pulling off the crime.

    d8Complication
    1You are wanted by the law. Guards keep an eye out for you for the next 1d4 downtime periods.†
    2A witness can identify you.†
    3You gain a criminal reputation, good or bad, among the local underworld.
    4Your target survives and seeks revenge.†
    5A local thieves' guild takes notice of your work and wants you to join, or pay them tribute.†
    6You find evidence implicating an important local figure in a separate crime.
    7Your target was under the protection of a rival criminal, who is now displeased with you.†
    8You accidentally steal something far more valuable, or far more dangerous, than you expected.

    † Might involve a rival.

    Gambling

    A character can spend downtime gambling — at cards, dice, or any other game of chance. This activity requires one workweek of effort plus a stake of at least 10 gp (up to 1,000 gp or more, as the player sees fit). The character makes three checks — a Wisdom (Insight) check, a Charisma (Deception) check, and a Charisma (Intimidation) check — each against its own DC, since each represents a different opponent or hand faced during the week. Proficiency with an appropriate gaming set can replace the relevant skill for any of the three checks.

    Each check rolls against its own DC of 5 + 2d10, representing a different opponent.

    Gambling Results

    SuccessesResult
    0Lose the entire bet, and accrue a debt equal to that amount.
    1Lose half the bet.
    2Gain the bet back plus half again (1.5x).
    3Gain twice the bet (2x).

    Gambling Complications

    Games of chance draw a colorful (and sometimes dangerous) crowd. There's a 10% chance of a complication for the workweek spent gambling.

    d6Complication
    1You're accused of cheating, whether or not you actually did.†
    2You attract the attention of a local gambling ring or crime boss.†
    3You win big against someone who can't afford to lose, and they resent you for it.†
    4A fellow gambler tries to recruit you into a scheme to cheat other players.
    5You make a valuable contact who enjoys your company at the table.
    6A brawl breaks out over the game. Succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity or Strength check (your choice) or take 1d6 damage.

    † Might involve a rival.

    Pit Fighting

    A character can spend downtime pit fighting — competing in organized (if not always legal) combat for money. This requires one workweek of effort and no gold up front. The character makes three checks — a Strength (Athletics) check, a Dexterity (Acrobatics) check, and a Constitution check (adding their largest Hit Die as a bonus, not rolling it) — each against its own DC, representing a different bout. A weapon attack roll can replace any one of the three checks.

    Each check rolls against its own DC of 5 + 2d10, representing a different bout.

    Pit Fighting Results

    SuccessesResult
    0Lose all your bouts, earning nothing.
    1Win 50 gp.
    2Win 100 gp.
    3Win 200 gp.

    Pit Fighting Complications

    The pit fighting circuit is a rough crowd. There's a 10% chance of a complication for the workweek spent fighting.

    d8Complication
    1You suffer a lasting injury. You have Disadvantage on Strength or Dexterity checks (your choice) until you finish a long rest after a week of recovery.
    2An opponent you beat holds a grudge against you.†
    3You're accused of cheating, whether or not you actually did.†
    4A promoter tries to recruit you into an illegal, high-stakes fighting circuit.†
    5You gain a following of fans, who now recognize you in public.
    6A gambler who bet against you is furious at your win, or delighted by your loss.†
    7You're offered a bribe to throw your next match.†
    8You catch the eye of a noble or wealthy patron who wants to sponsor you.

    † Might involve a rival.

    Research

    A character can spend downtime researching a topic — a piece of lore, a rumor, a person, or anything else that requires digging through records, questioning contacts, or poring over old texts. Research costs at least 50 gp, requires access to a library, sage, or other useful resource, and takes one workweek. The character makes an Intelligence check. If they have access to a well-stocked library or a knowledgeable sage, the check is made with Advantage.

    Gold bonus: +0 (+1 per 100 gp spent beyond the base 50 gp, capped at +6)

    Research Results

    Check TotalResult
    1-5No useful information is found.
    6-10The character learns one piece of information relevant to their research.
    11-20The character learns two pieces of information relevant to their research.
    21+The character learns three pieces of information relevant to their research.

    Research Complications

    Digging into old records and rumors doesn't always stay quiet. There's a 10% chance of a complication for the workweek spent researching.

    d6Complication
    1You draw the attention of someone else interested in the same topic.†
    2The information you find is outdated, incomplete, or subtly wrong.
    3You discover a secret someone would rather keep hidden.†
    4Your research is confiscated or destroyed before you can finish.
    5A librarian, sage, or archivist takes a personal interest in your work and wants to help (or hinder) further.
    6You uncover a lead pointing toward a much larger mystery.

    † Might involve a rival.
